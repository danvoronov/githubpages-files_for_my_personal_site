At some point YouTube recommended me a Switch Angel video, I hit play and was surprised. She play music with code — the code right there on screen. Then I started watching her channel and other videos, and then somehow stopped. I knew **algorithmic music** existed, but knowing is one thing, and seeing a person do it virtuosically and harmoniously is another.

I'd want to play like that too. The system is called **Strudel** https://strudel.cc/, it's live coding, and you can not only write a "program" and run it — Switch Angel keeps adding to and changing the code right during the performance, meaning she plays music while writing code, like on an instrument. 

It's similar to when I played electronic music in Ableton app and performed: my sounds were pre-made, but I played live. Hers is even more interesting because she doesn't control things with a mouse — she just writes text.

On one hand there's **accessibility** here. No MIDI controller needed, nothing to buy or install. It's JavaScript in the browser: open it, write code, and music sounds right away. So it's a very democratic instrument. The limitation here is precisely the learning curve, in my head. So on the other hand, I tried to start a few times but got stuck on the basics. Properly speaking, you have to learn the list of commands.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HMUZ01p0akw

What inspires me is that such people exist. She's been in this community for a long time and makes tools for it. This is an interview with a person, not just with a musician: by her very **existence** she shows that the instrument is cool. She publishes the code of all her songs so that the practice is visible, grows and expands.

In the video she goes from a skate park session in Boston to a live club show, where the host gets a crash course in Strudel: type drum patterns and note numbers, hit play, iterate live.

She also carries the idea that right now **algorithms** subjugate people and eat attention: we all see a bunch of TikTok zombies in public space — people are absent. She does the opposite, she subjugates algorithms, writes music with their help, publishes open code. She uses algorithms to create beauty.
